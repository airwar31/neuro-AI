# 🔥 Autoimmune Encephalitis

**Autoimmune Encephalitis** encompasses a group of acute and subacute inflammatory disorders of the brain caused by autoantibodies directed against neuronal cell-surface synaptic receptors, ion channels, or intracellular antigens.

---

## 🧬 Major Clinical Syndromes

1. **Anti-NMDA Receptor Encephalitis (Anti-NMDAR)**:
   - Antibodies target the GluN1 subunit of NMDA receptors, causing receptor capping, internalization, and hypofunction.
   - Demographics: High prevalence in young females; frequently paraneoplastic associated with **ovarian teratomas**.
   - Classic Stages:
     1. Viral-like prodrome (headache, low-grade fever).
     2. Psychiatric phase (acute behavioral changes, visual/auditory hallucinations, mania, catatonia).
     3. Neurological phase (orofacial-lingual dyskinesias, choreoathetosis, autonomic instability, central hypoventilation).
2. **Anti-LGI1 Encephalitis**:
   - Pathognomonic feature: **Faciobrachial Dystonic Seizures (FBDS)** — brief, unilateral jerking movements of the arm and ipsilateral face occurring dozens of times daily.
   - Severe refractory hyponatremia.
3. **Anti-CASPR2 Encephalitis**:
   - Manifests as Morvan syndrome (encephalopathy, insomnia, hyperhidrosis, autonomic storm) and peripheral nerve hyperexcitability (neuromyotonia).

---

## 🧲 Diagnosis and Treatment
- **Brain MRI** (see [[MRI_Biomarkers_and_Neuroimaging]]): T2/FLAIR hyperintensity in bilateral medial temporal lobes and limbic structures.
- **CSF Analysis** (see [[Laboratory_and_CSF_Biomarkers]]): Lymphocytic pleocytosis and positive cell-based antibody titers.
- **Immunotherapy**: High-dose corticosteroids, IVIG, plasma exchange, and second-line rituximab or cyclophosphamide.
