# 🧬 Huntington's Disease (HD)

**Huntington's Disease** is an autosomal dominant, fully penetrant neurodegenerative disorder caused by an unstable expansion of cytosine-adenine-guanine (**CAG**) trinucleotide repeats in the *HTT* gene on chromosome 4, encoding the huntingtin protein.

---

## 🔬 Genetics and Molecular Pathology
- **Normal Alleles**: ≤ 26 CAG repeats.
- **Intermediate**: 27–35 CAG repeats (normal phenotype, risk of expansion in gametes).
- **Reduced Penetrance**: 36–39 CAG repeats.
- **Full Penetrance**: ≥ 40 CAG repeats (repeat length inversely correlates with age of onset).
- Mutant huntingtin (mHTT) causes selective transcriptional dysregulation, loss of BDNF trophic support, and apoptosis of striatal medium spiny GABAergic neurons in the caudate and putamen.

---

## 🩺 Clinical Triad
1. **Chorea and Motor Disturbances**:
   - Involuntary, irregular, jerky, non-stereotyped movements ("dance-like" chorea).
   - In advanced stages, chorea gives way to severe bradykinesia, dystonia, and rigidity (Westphal variant).
2. **Cognitive Deterioration**:
   - Subcortical executive dementia (slowed processing, mental inflexibility, perseveration).
3. **Psychiatric Manifestations**:
   - Irritability, apathy, major depressive disorder, high suicide risk, psychosis.

---

## 🧲 Neuroimaging Markers
From [[MRI_Biomarkers_and_Neuroimaging]]:
- Severe bilateral atrophy of the **caudate nuclei heads (caput nuclei caudati)**.
- Secondary "boxcar" dilatation of the frontal horns of the lateral ventricles (increased bicaudate ratio).
