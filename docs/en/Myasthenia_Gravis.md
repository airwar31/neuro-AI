# ⚡ Myasthenia Gravis (MG)

**Myasthenia Gravis** is the prototypical autoimmune disease of the neuromuscular junction (NMJ), characterized by antibody-mediated destruction of postsynaptic signaling machinery, leading to **pathological, exercise-induced fatigable muscle weakness**.

---

## 🔬 Pathophysiology
- In 80–85% of cases, antibodies target the **Nicotinic Acetylcholine Receptor (AChR-Ab)**.
- Complement activation (MAC formation) flattens postsynaptic junctional folds, reducing available receptor density and safety margin for neuromuscular transmission.
- In 5–10% of cases, antibodies target **Muscle-Specific Kinase (MuSK-Ab)**, resulting in severe bulbar, facial, and respiratory muscle weakness without complement activation.
- Thymic Pathology: 65% exhibit thymic follicular hyperplasia; 10–15% harbor a **thymoma**.

---

## 🩺 Clinical Presentations
- **Ocular Myasthenia**: Asymmetric ptosis (drooping eyelid) worsening with sustained upgaze, and binocular diplopia.
- **Generalized Myasthenia**: Proximal limb weakness (difficulty climbing stairs, combing hair), neck extensor weakness ("dropped head syndrome").
- **Bulbar Myasthenia**: Dysarthria with nasal vocal fatigue, dysphagia, and chewing fatigue.
- **Myasthenic Crisis**: Acute weakness of diaphragmatic and intercostal respiratory muscles necessitating mechanical ventilation.

---

## 📋 Diagnostic Tests
- Ice Pack Test: Application of an ice pack to the ptotic eyelid for 2 minutes improves ptosis by ≥2 mm (due to cold-induced reduction of acetylcholinesterase activity).
- Electromyography: Repetitive nerve stimulation (RNS) at 3 Hz demonstrates >10% decremental response in compound muscle action potential (CMAP) amplitude.
- Serological testing for AChR and MuSK antibodies.
- Contrast chest CT/MRI to evaluate for thymoma.
