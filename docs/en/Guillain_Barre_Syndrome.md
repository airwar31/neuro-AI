# 🚶 Guillain-Barré Syndrome (GBS)

**Guillain-Barré Syndrome** is an acute, immune-mediated inflammatory polyradiculoneuropathy characterized by rapidly progressive, symmetrical ascending flaccid paresis and global hypo/areflexia.

---

## 🔬 Pathophysiology and Molecular Mimicry
- In approximately two-thirds of cases, onset is preceded (1–3 weeks) by an infectious episode, most frequently **Campylobacter jejuni** enteritis, CMV, EBV, or COVID-19.
- Lipooligosaccharides on the bacterial coat of *C. jejuni* share molecular homology with gangliosides in human peripheral nerves (GM1, GD1a, GQ1b).
- Autoreactive antibodies bind to Schwann cell myelin (in AIDP — Acute Inflammatory Demyelinating Polyneuropathy) or axolemma at the nodes of Ranvier (in AMAN / AMSAN axonal variants).

---

## 🩺 Clinical Features
- **Symmetrical Ascending Paresis**: Weakness begins in the lower extremities and progresses rostrally to involve trunk, upper limbs, and cranial nerves ("Landry's ascending paralysis").
- **Areflexia**: Generalized loss of deep tendon reflexes.
- **Facial Diplegia**: Bilateral facial nerve palsy in ~50% of patients.
- **Respiratory Compromise**: Neuromuscular respiratory failure occurs in 25–30% of cases, requiring ICU monitoring.
- **Autonomic Instability**: Cardiac arrhythmias, labile blood pressure, paralytic ileus.

---

## 🧪 Laboratory Biomarkers
According to [[Laboratory_and_CSF_Biomarkers]]:
- **Albuminocytological Dissociation in CSF**: Marked elevation in CSF total protein (>1.0 g/L) with normal white blood cell count (<10 cells/µL). Characteristically develops during the second week of illness.
- **Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS)**: Conduction blocks, temporal dispersion, and prolonged distal motor latencies.
