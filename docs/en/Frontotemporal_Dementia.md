# 🎭 Frontotemporal Dementia (FTD)

**Frontotemporal Dementia (Pick's Disease)** encompasses a spectrum of neurodegenerative syndromes characterized by selective degeneration of the frontal and anterior temporal lobes. Unlike [[Alzheimers_Disease|Alzheimer's Disease]], FTD typically presents in middle age (45–65 years) with prominent behavioral changes or language deficits, while visuospatial orientation and episodic memory remain relatively intact early on.

---

## 🧩 Clinical Subtypes

1. **Behavioral Variant FTD (bvFTD)**:
   - Early behavioral disinhibition (inappropriate social conduct, loss of etiquette).
   - Early apathy and loss of initiative.
   - Early loss of sympathy and empathy.
   - Hyperorality and dietary changes (binging on carbohydrates and sweets).
   - Stereotypical, compulsive, or ritualistic behaviors.
   - Executive dysfunction on neuropsychological testing.

2. **Primary Progressive Aphasia (PPA)**:
   - **Semantic Variant (svPPA)**: Loss of word and object meaning, surface dyslexia, preserved speech fluency.
   - **Nonfluent / Agrammatic Variant (nfvPPA)**: Agrammatism in language production, effortful halting speech, speech apraxia.

---

## 🧲 Neuroimaging Markers
According to [[MRI_Biomarkers_and_Neuroimaging]]:
- Severe, often asymmetric "knife-edge" cortical atrophy restricted to the prefrontal cortex and anterior temporal poles.
- Relative preservation of the posterior parietal lobes and early preservation of hippocampal volume, differentiating FTD from [[Alzheimers_Disease|Alzheimer's Disease]].
